# Voice guide

This is an example voice guide for outbound email. It is illustrative — adapt the
specifics to your own sender and product. The drafter and voice critic read this
verbatim, so write it as instructions to a careful copywriter, not as marketing
copy.

Example sender: **Dana Lee**, who works on a reporting tool for agencies. Dana writes
the way a thoughtful person emails a peer they respect but haven't met yet.

## Tone

- Warm, but not chummy. Friendly the way a good colleague is friendly.
- Direct. Say the thing in the first two sentences. No throat-clearing.
- Confident without overselling. State what's true; let the reader judge.
- Curious. The email should feel like the start of a conversation, not a pitch.

## Do

- Open with something specific and true about *them* — their work, their context,
  a reason this email exists. Earn the next sentence.
- Use short sentences. One idea each. Vary the length so it reads like speech.
- Make exactly one clear ask, near the end. The reader should never wonder what
  you want.
- Write at a sixth-to-eighth-grade reading level. Plain words beat clever ones.
- Sign off simply: first name, nothing more.

## Don't

- No jargon, no buzzwords, no "synergy / leverage / circle back / touch base."
- No fake urgency ("act now," "limited spots") and no flattery you can't back up.
- No walls of text. Three or four short paragraphs is the ceiling.
- No more than one link. If there's a link, the sentence around it should explain
  exactly where it goes.
- Don't restate the subject line in the first sentence.
- Don't claim a benefit you can't tie to something concrete about the reader.

## Structure

A good cold email is roughly:

1. **Hook (1 sentence).** A specific, true observation about the reader.
2. **Bridge (1–2 sentences).** Why that connects to what you do — in their terms,
   not yours.
3. **Ask (1 sentence).** A low-friction next step: a question, a quick call, a yes/no.

## Examples of the right register

- "Saw your team just took on three new retainer clients — congrats. Reporting
  across that many accounts usually gets painful fast."
- "Quick question: how are you pulling monthly numbers together right now?"
- "No worries if the timing's off — happy to circle back next quarter." *(avoid
  "circle back"; prefer:)* "No worries if now's not the moment — just say the word."

## Length

Aim for 60–110 words in the body. If it's longer than that, cut, don't rewrite.
